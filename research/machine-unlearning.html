When “forget this data” breaks the model

Machine unlearning removes the influence of some training examples without retraining from scratch. I studied a popular shortcut, NegGrad+, and found that it becomes unreliable when the examples to forget all come from the same class.

What is settled

  • In these BatchNorm networks, concentrated deletion requests make NegGrad+ unreliable; spread ones of the same size do not.
  • The effect goes beyond the difficulty of the classes involved, and is far stronger under NegGrad+ than under a bounded control.
  • The fixes tried (BatchNorm changes, a bounded target, per-class tuning) protect accuracy, but mostly by forgetting less.

What is still open

  • Why: BatchNorm is the main suspect, but no study isolated a mechanism.
  • Whether any method can forget a concentrated request as well as retraining does, without breaking the model.
  • Whether any of this measures real erasure or privacy: it was never tested.
  • How far it generalises beyond a small CNN on Fashion-MNIST.

What I would do next (ideas, none of them run)

  1. Run the PCGrad study already frozen on 17 October: does resolving conflicts between the “keep” and “forget” gradients improve the trade-off?
  2. Swap BatchNorm for GroupNorm or LayerNorm: the most direct test of the BatchNorm suspicion.
  3. Measure forgetting properly for the fixes (membership inference, distance to the retrained model), since they may only look good because they act weakly.
  4. Try other unlearning methods on concentrated requests, to see whether the problem is specific to NegGrad+.
  5. Predict risky requests: check on existing data whether simple batch statistics, computed before unlearning, flag the requests that will fail.
  6. Close the gaps of the audit: replay the original study with recalibrated evaluation, and recover the raw Tiny ImageNet tables.

The basics, from zero

Everything you need to follow the rest of the page, with no prior knowledge: what the model is, how it learns, what BatchNorm does, and the statistics behind every number.

1 · An image classifier gives a score to each class

The model looks at a 28×28 grey image and outputs 10 numbers, one per class (t-shirt, trouser, …, ankle boot). These raw scores are called logits. The softmax turns them into probabilities that add up to 1: pk = ezk / Σj ezj. The error on one image is the cross-entropy −log ptrue: close to 0 when the model is confident and right, very large when it gives the true class a tiny probability.

Try it: three classes

2 · The network used: a small CNN with BatchNorm

A convolutional neural network slides small 3×3 filters over the image to detect local patterns (edges, then textures, then shapes). The one used in almost every study has 1.7 million parameters. Click a layer.

The original model O is trained for 15 passes over the 55,000 images (epochs), in batches of 128, with stochastic gradient descent (learning rate 0.05 decreasing along a cosine curve, momentum 0.9, weight decay 5·10⁻⁴). It reaches about 93% accuracy on the 10,000 test images. A fresh O is trained for every seed.

3 · How a network learns, and how unlearning hijacks it

Training repeats one move thousands of times: compute the error on a batch, compute its gradient (the direction in which each parameter would increase the error), and take a small step the other way: θ ← θ − η·gradient, where η is the learning rate. Unlearning reuses the same machinery for one epoch (425 steps) on the data to keep, in batches of 128, each step paired with a batch of 64 images to forget:

  • Fine-tuning (reference): keep training on the data to keep only.
  • NegGrad+: minimise α·error(keep) − (1−α)·error(forget), i.e. also climb the error on the data to forget. The forget weight 1−α is small (0.001, 0.01 or 0.05) because climbing is dangerous.
  • Safety belt: if the gradient is longer than 5, it is shortened to 5 (gradient clipping).
  • Retrained model R: trained from scratch without the forgotten images; the ideal everyone tries to approach.

4 · BatchNorm, and why a one-class batch is special

Inside the network, each channel of each convolution produces numbers whose scale drifts during training. A BatchNorm layer recentres and rescales them using the statistics of the current batch: x̂ = (x − mean) / √(variance + ε), then multiplies and shifts by two learned numbers. During training it uses the batch's own mean and variance and slowly updates a stored average (stored ← 0.9·stored + 0.1·batch); at test time it uses the stored average.

Try it: one channel, one batch of 16 images

Illustration: the 16 values are made up to show the arithmetic; the real network has 32 to 128 channels per layer.

Two consequences matter for this project. During NegGrad+, a one-class forget batch is normalised by its own unusual statistics, and the gradient flows through them. And the forget batches also pull the stored averages towards one class: if you then evaluate the model with those stored statistics, it can look broken. Recalibration means recomputing the stored statistics exactly on the data to keep, without touching any weight; most results here use it.

5 · The statistics, with the real data

Every study is repeated with several seeds: a seed fixes all the randomness (initial weights, image order, which images are forgotten), so each seed is an independent rerun with its own model. One seed gives one number; the statistics are done on these numbers. Below are the 12 real values of the tuning study's penalty (how many more accuracy points concentrated requests cost than spread ones). Change how many seeds are used.

Mean = average of the seeds. SD = how much seeds differ. Standard error SE = SD/√n, the uncertainty of the mean. The 95% confidence interval (CI) is mean ± t × SE, where t comes from Student's distribution with n−1 degrees of freedom. The t-statistic = mean / SE; the p-value is the chance of a t at least that large if the true penalty were 0. Equivalence (TOST): the 90% interval must lie inside a margin fixed in advance (±2 points). A result can be both “detected” (CI excludes 0) and “equivalent” (inside the margin): a real but small effect. (The values are rounded to 2 decimals here, so t may differ from the frozen analysis, 11.95, in the last digit.)

  • Why seeds, not images? The 11 runs of one seed share the same model, so they are not independent; counting them separately would fake precision.
  • Holm correction: when a study runs several tests, p-values are adjusted so that the chance of any false alarm stays at 5%.
  • Power: before a study, thousands of fake studies are simulated to find how many seeds are needed to detect a given effect at least 80% of the time. Calibration: the same simulations without any effect check that the test raises false alarms only about 5% of the time.

6 · The concentration model: entropy and power means

To separate “concentrated” from “made of hard classes”, two tools were needed.

Label entropy: how mixed is a forget set?

Power mean: many ways to combine two classes

Suppose class A alone gives an early forget loss of 1 and class B alone gives 9. What should a 50/50 mix give? It depends on how difficulties combine; the power mean Mλ = (½·1λ + ½·9λ)1/λ covers a whole family.

The study fitted, for each seed, early loss = log₁₀ Mλ(class mix) + γ·entropy + noise, letting λ take its best value between −3 and 3. Whatever the combination rule, something was left over: γ = −0.246 decade per bit. Going from 1 class (0 bits) to 10 classes (3.32 bits) therefore lowers the early forget loss by about 0.246 × 3.32 ≈ 0.82 decade, a factor of about 6.6, beyond what the classes themselves predict.

The setting

A small convolutional network is trained on Fashion-MNIST (55,000 photos of clothes, 10 classes). Then 600 of these photos must be forgotten. The gold standard is a model retrained from scratch without them; approximate methods try to get close to it much faster.

The question is simple: with the same number of photos to forget, does it matter whether they all come from one class (concentrated) or are spread evenly over the ten classes (spread)? In the concentrated case, the other photos of that class stay in the training data: the model should forget these 600 boots, not boots in general.

Build a deletion request

During unlearning, the photos to forget are fed to the network in batches of 64. Each BatchNorm layer rescales its signals using the mean and variance of the current batch. A concentrated request therefore produces batches made of a single class, whose statistics are very different from those of a normal, mixed batch. That turned out to matter.

Why NegGrad+ can run away

NegGrad+ minimises α·CE(keep) − (1−α)·CE(forget): it keeps practising on the data that stays, and pushes the error on the data to forget up. The error used is the cross-entropy, CE = −log p, where p is the probability the model gives to the true class.

Push the probability of the true class down

As p goes to 0, −log p grows without limit: nothing stops the push. In the experiments, a forget loss above 10 counts as a divergence. A control method that instead teaches random wrong labels has a finite target and cannot run away in the same way.

What I found

1 · Concentration makes NegGrad+ unstable

In a prospective study with 10 independent repetitions (seeds) and 68 mixes of 1, 2, 5 or 10 classes, NegGrad+ diverged mostly when the request was concentrated. Hover the bars.

Runs out of: 100 one-class, 450 two-class, 120 five-class and 10 ten-class sets (implementation A, weight 0.01). A variant that keeps BatchNorm in evaluation mode (C) never diverged or collapsed in this study.

2 · It is not just that some classes are harder

A statistical model accounted for the difficulty of each class and for a whole family of ways to combine them (power means of order −3 to 3). Even then, less concentrated sets had a lower early forget loss than their classes predict: −0.246 decade per bit of label entropy (95% CI −0.263 to −0.228, all 10 seeds negative). A decade is a factor of 10. This rules out the combination rules of that family, not every possible interaction between specific pairs of classes.

3 · It is specific to how NegGrad+ forgets

With everything else identical, I compared NegGrad+ with the random-label control. The measure is the typical size of the gradients before they are clipped (Ḡ, in decades): how much more it grows for concentrated than for spread requests.

Inside the tuning study

For each class, 20 settings (4 learning rates × 5 forget weights) were tried on 3 seeds. A setting counted as stable if no run diverged or collapsed, and as feasible if it also reached the forgetting strength of the retrained model. Pick a class.

Reading. Tuning brought the penalty from about 14 points to about 1, but for 7 of 10 classes no stable setting reached the retrained model's strength: the tuned method mostly acted less. The BatchNorm fix behaved similarly: it protected the model but barely changed its predictions on the data to forget.

A measurement trap

Many runs looked “collapsed” (test accuracy ≤ 20%). But that accuracy is measured with the statistics BatchNorm stored during unlearning, which the single-class batches had corrupted. Recompute those statistics on the remaining data, without touching any weight, and see what happens.

A real loss remains (about 74% vs 93% for the original model), but much smaller than it first appeared. How an unlearned model is evaluated can change the conclusion.

Every study, in order

The project is a chain of 13 studies. Each one answered a question left open by the previous one. Open a study to see its question, its design, what came out and what it does not show. Filter by role or by status.

Post hoc: the analysis was decided after seeing data. Pre-specified: question, test and wording fixed and committed before the run. Exploratory: pre-specified, but with fewer seeds than the protocol itself requires for a firm claim.

01The original study (v3): a success for the wrong reasoncriterion fixed in advance · 5 seedsFashion-MNIST, CNN, concentrated vs random forget sets
Question
Does NegGrad+ “forget better” when the 600 examples to forget come from a single class, compared with a random set of the same size? The directional criterion was written down before the run.
Design
A small BatchNorm CNN trained on 55,000 Fashion-MNIST images; fine-tuning and NegGrad+ compared with a model retrained without the forget set, over 5 seeds, with hyperparameters tuned beforehand.
Result
The criterion was met, but only through runs that collapsed: 26 of 55 runs ended at ≤ 20% test accuracy, all with concentrated forget sets.
Does not show
Any forgetting. A model that recognises nothing “forgets” trivially. Accuracy was measured with the BatchNorm statistics left by unlearning, and no recalibrated evaluation exists for this study, so we cannot tell how much of the collapse was in the weights.
02Diagnostics 2–3: is it the BatchNorm treatment?post hoc · 5 seedsWhich way of handling BatchNorm avoids the blow-up?
Question
The concentrated runs looked like an optimisation blow-up. Does it depend on how BatchNorm normalises the batch to forget?
Design
Same model, weight 1−α = 0.01, 30 concentrated tasks. Implementation A (separate training-mode passes, the original) compared with B (retained and forgotten images in one batch) and C (BatchNorm frozen in evaluation mode). A third diagnostic tested E1 (forget batch normalised with running statistics of the retained data) on one class. Reading criteria were dated before each computation, but the classes were chosen after v3.
Result
A diverged in 29 of 30 tasks; B and C in 0 of 30, with test accuracy within about 1.5 points of the original model. E1 did not diverge on class “Trouser” (0/5 seeds; 92.4–93.3% test accuracy vs 93.0–93.4% for the original model).
Does not show
That BatchNorm is the cause: each variant changes several things, the weight was fixed, and later studies found that these variants fail elsewhere (studies 05 and 06).
03Replication v1.3: does it hold with new seeds, and on CIFAR-10?pre-specified, exploratory · 5 + 3 seedsA vs C on all 10 classes and 3 weights
Question
With newly derived seeds, draws and models, are concentrated runs more unstable than spread ones, under A and under C?
Design
Fashion-MNIST (CNN, 5 seeds) and CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, 3 seeds); 10 classes × 3 weights (0.001, 0.01, 0.05). Main statistic: the share of concentrated runs more unstable than the paired spread run (a numerical failure counts as more unstable; otherwise the higher mean forget loss). Protocol committed before the run, but the analysis code was not.
Result
Fashion-MNIST: share 0.86 under A (Holm p = 1.7·10⁻⁵); difference A − C 0.37 (Holm p = 1.5·10⁻⁴). At 0.01, A diverged in 30/50 concentrated runs and collapsed in 24/50; C in 0. CIFAR-10: the share was 1 in all 3 seeds (test not computable), A − C 0.43 (Holm p = 0.0039); A diverged in 60 runs and never collapsed, which shows that divergence and collapse are different things. Spread runs never diverged.
Does not show
That concentration itself matters: the six classes that diverged were exactly those that had collapsed in v3, so class identity and concentration were still confounded. With 5 and 3 seeds the protocol classifies everything as exploratory.
04NegGrad+ vs random labels, first try: the clipping burdenpre-specified · 10 seeds (5 reused)Is the effect specific to NegGrad+?
Question
Is the concentration effect specific to NegGrad+'s unbounded “push up”, or does it also appear with a bounded objective?
Design
Both objectives share the original model, forget sets, batch streams, forward passes, BatchNorm treatment and optimiser. The control (RL) teaches labels drawn once among the other classes. Measure: the share of steps whose gradient had to be clipped (threshold 5). Contrast: concentration effect under NG minus under RL, per seed.
Result
At 0.01 the difference was 0.304 (95% CI 0.289–0.318, p < 10⁻¹⁰). But RL sat at the floor: 4 clipped steps in 330 runs.
Does not show
That RL is unaffected: a measure stuck at zero cannot see small effects. This motivated a continuous measure (study 08).
05BatchNorm study v1.0: which part of BatchNorm matters?pre-specified, exploratory · 5 seedsA vs E1 vs E1D, 600 runs
Question
Does the instability come from the gradient flowing through the batch statistics, from the statistics themselves, or from neither?
Design
A (own statistics, gradient through them), E1D (own statistics but detached, no buffer update) and E1 (running statistics of retained data). 150 concentrated and 50 spread runs per weight; 600 runs, no numerical failure. Shares the split, classes and weights of v1.3, so it is a follow-up, not an independent replication. Two engineering amendments were committed before the main study.
Result
Divergences on concentrated sets at 0.001 / 0.01 / 0.05: A 12 / 90 / 150, E1D 12 / 89 / 121, E1 0 / 60 / 150 (and E1 diverged in 47/50 spread runs at 0.05). On the optimised forget loss, A was above E1D in 82% of concentrated runs (Holm p = 3.7·10⁻⁵; +0.83 decade), more than on spread sets (+0.33, Holm p = 4.0·10⁻⁴), with a gap growing with the weight (+0.01, +0.75, +1.73 decade).
Does not show
A single mechanism: the results fit a weight-dependent contribution of the gradient through the batch statistics, but E1D and E1 still diverged widely, so that is not the whole story.
06Control of C on CIFAR-10: does freezing BatchNorm cost accuracy?pre-specified, exploratory · 3 seedsResNet-18, 33 cells per weight
Question
C avoided divergence on CIFAR-10. Does it pay for it with accuracy, and is that loss real or an evaluation artefact?
Design
C and E1 on 33 condition × weight cells at 0.001 and 0.01, compared with fine-tuning; evaluation with native and with exactly recalibrated BatchNorm statistics.
Result

07Concentration beyond class identity: 68 mixespre-specified, exploratory · 10 new seedsThe study that separates concentration from class difficulty
Question
Is the effect due to concentration, or just to some classes being hard?
Design
Seeds 401–410, each with its own original model, 68 compositions per seed at n = 600 and weight 0.01: the 10 one-class sets, all 45 two-class sets, 12 five-class sets and the spread set. Outcome: the early forget loss (mean log₁₀ forget loss over steps 0–23), chosen beforehand because it separated later-diverging runs on earlier data (AUC 0.92). Model: per seed, S = log₁₀ Mλ(class proportions; class effects) + γ·H + noise, where Mλ is a power mean of order λ ∈ [−3, 3] and H the label entropy in bits. Test on γ over seeds, with a threshold calibrated by parametric bootstrap (2,000 draws).
Result
γ = −0.246 decade per bit (95% CI −0.263 to −0.228, p = 0.001, all 10 seeds negative; estimated λ between 0.34 and 1.09). Less concentrated sets had a lower early loss than their classes predict, for every combination rule of that family. Under A, 62/100 one-class and 67/450 two-class runs diverged, none with 5 or 10 classes. Under C (secondary), γ = −0.004, equivalent to zero at ±0.075 for the estimated λ but positive with λ fixed at 0.
Does not show
That no pair of classes interacts in some special way (the model cannot represent that), nor anything about divergence or forgetting directly: the outcome is the early loss level, at one weight and for one model.
08NegGrad+ vs random labels, done properly: a continuous measurepre-specified · 10 new seedsḠ, the mean log size of the gradients before clipping
Question
Same as study 04, with a measure that cannot get stuck at zero.
Design
Seeds 101–110, new models, nothing reused. Ḡ = mean over steps of log₁₀ of the gradient norm before clipping. The analysis was calibrated by Monte Carlo before any run (false-positive rate of the main test 0.044–0.059; Holm family 0.036–0.044). One amendment, on a unit-test tolerance, followed a pilot stop that came before any unlearning run. 782 units; replays bitwise identical.
Result
At 0.01: concentration raised Ḡ by 0.705 decade under NegGrad+ and by 0.053 under random labels; difference 0.652 (95% CI 0.607–0.696, p < 10⁻¹⁰, all 10 seeds). At 0.001 the difference was slightly negative (−0.015); at 0.05 it was 2.644, with the random-label shift negative. Concentrated NG runs lost 1.3, 46.8 and 82.9 test-accuracy points at the three weights, RL only 1.0, 2.0 and 2.9.
Does not show
That Ḡ measures instability (its direction depends on the weight), nor that random labels are unstable: their small shift never came with divergence.

Question
Does the gradient-size signal of study 08 appear outside Fashion-MNIST?
Design
Ten target classes fixed before downloading the archive; for each, a concentrated set of 50 images paired with a spread set (one image of the class and one from each of 49 others). 10The source of the statistics alone (v1.2)pre-specified, exploratory · 10 new seedsE1D vs RD64: only where the statistics come from changes
Question
With the gradient path held fixed, does it matter whether the batch to forget is normalised with its own statistics or with those of the concurrent retained batch?
Design
Seeds 411–420, the 68 compositions of study 07. E1D and RD64 both detach the statistics; they differ only in their source. Outcome: capped early loss burden over steps 0–19. Main contrast θ: how much RD64 − E1D changes for mixed sets relative to one-class sets, on the training pass (H1) then on a reference pass (H2). A calibration simulation run before the study showed that a test meant to isolate concentration (Δγ) gave 8.2–19.3% false positives under combination rules outside the family, so it was withdrawn and reported only descriptively. A concentration-specific effect or a mechanism: the comparison does not separate the source of the statistics from the mismatch between the batch and the statistics used.
11A frozen alternative target (CF)pre-specified · 12 new seedsReplace the unbounded push with a bounded target
Question
If NegGrad+ fails because its push is unbounded, does descending towards a fixed, sensible target avoid the concentration penalty?
Design
For each forgotten (x, y), the target is the original model's softmax over the classes other than y (true class masked before the softmax). NG at weights 0.01 and 0.05, RL and CF at 0.01, 0.05 and 0.2; seeds 421–432. Outcome U: accuracy loss on the retained data, after recalibrating BatchNorm on that data. Two main tests with Holm correction. The draft planned 10 seeds; the power simulation gave 0.72 (below 0.80), so 12 seeds were fixed before the freeze (0.81). One amendment: a unit test compared logits computed with different batch sizes, which differ under the A100's TF32 arithmetic. 1,467 units, replays bitwise identical.
Result
Interaction NG vs CF: 16.2 points (95% CI 14.1–18.4, Holm p = 7·10⁻⁹), all 12 seeds. RL vs CF: −0.02 (no difference detected; no equivalence test planned). CF's own penalty 0.25 point, detected but inside ±2 points. NG collapsed in 70/120 concentrated runs at 0.01 and 117/120 at 0.05; RL and CF never.
Does not show
Successful forgetting: the force check rested on a nearly null reference (NG force 0.019 decade, CF 0.051), and on concentrated sets CF left the accuracy on the forgotten images at 96.7–97.4% (fine-tuning 98.2%), even though it fitted its targets during training.
12A bundled BatchNorm fix (RD64)pre-specified · 14 new seedsNormalise the batch to forget with retained-batch statistics
Question
Does the RD64 treatment, which never diverged in study 10, reduce the concentration penalty on retained utility?
Design
At each BatchNorm layer, the batch to forget uses the mean and variance of the first 64 rows of the retained batch of the same step, detached, without updating the stored statistics. So three things change at once: the source of the statistics, the gradient path and the buffer updates. Seeds 433–446. Single test on I = penalty(A) − penalty(RD64). Power planned for a negative correlation between the two penalties: 12 seeds gave 0.737, 14 gave 0.818–0.872. No amendment.
Result
I = 13.7 points (95% CI 12.6–14.7, p = 4·10⁻¹³), positive in all 14 seeds (10.7–17.1). NG-A: 84/140 divergences and 75/140 collapses; NG-RD: 0 and 0. Accuracy on the concentrated forget sets (recalibrated): 80.2% NG-A, 96.7% NG-RD, 98.5% fine-tuning, 93.1% retrained.
Does not show
Which of the three changes matters, or forgetting: NG-RD's forgetting strength was about 0.03 decade, and its predictions on the forgotten images barely moved away from fine-tuning (1.7 points).
13Per-class tuning: does a good setting suffice?pre-specified · 3 + 12 new seeds20 settings per class, chosen by a fixed rule
Question
Maybe NegGrad+ was simply badly tuned for concentrated requests. Does the penalty survive when the learning rate and the forget weight are chosen separately for each class?
Design
Tuning seeds 447–449: grid of 4 learning rates (0.001–0.03) × 5 weights (0.001–0.05). A setting is stable if no run failed, diverged or collapsed, and feasible if it also reaches the forgetting strength of the retrained model. Rule: the feasible setting with the best validation accuracy, else the strongest stable one; it never sees test data or evaluation seeds, and the analysis recomputed it identically. Evaluation seeds 450–461 (12, fixed by power simulation): chosen setting vs the default 0.01/0.01. Main test plus a ±2-point equivalence test. Ran in two Colab sessions (A100 40 GB, then 80 GB).
Result
Penalty with tuning 1.07 points (95% CI 0.88–1.27), equivalent to zero within ±2; with the default setting 14.2. Divergences on concentrated sets 2/120 vs 71/120. But only 3 of 10 classes had a feasible setting; spread requests were stable under all 20 settings, concentrated ones under 1 to 16.
Does not show
An equal-strength comparison: for 7 classes the chosen setting acted less than the retrained model, and on the evaluation seeds the retrained strength was reached in only 20% of concentrated runs (67% of spread runs).

How it was done

It began with an unexpected failure, continued with exploratory diagnostics, and then moved to studies whose plan was fixed before any result was seen:

  • Protocol: the question, one main test and the wording of each possible conclusion.
  • Simulations: how many seeds are needed (power), and whether the test raises false alarms (calibration).
  • Freeze: the plan and the code are committed with a fingerprint before the run.
  • Pilot: only to measure computing time; its results stay sealed.
  • Study and frozen analysis: run on a cloud GPU (A100), analysed exactly as written.

Each seed trains a new model, and the statistics treat one seed as one data point. The studies share some data, so they are not fully independent replications, and the plans live in a private repository rather than a public registry.

When something went wrong

Three times (studies 08, 10 and 11) a pilot stopped on a blocking unit test before any unlearning run; in the last two the tolerance was too strict for the A100's reduced-precision (TF32) arithmetic. Each time the fix was written as a dated amendment, committed, and the pilot re-run; no result had been looked at. The notebooks also stop on their own if, for example, the code fingerprint differs from the frozen one, or if a replayed run is not bit-for-bit identical.

How the results were checked

Before writing anything up, I audited the four most recent studies end to end:

  • Code: the code executed on the GPU had the same fingerprint as the frozen, committed notebook, in all four studies; no blocking check failed.
  • Analyses: six frozen analyses were re-run from the raw per-run tables and reproduced their committed outputs exactly.
  • Numbers: 52 key numbers quoted in the write-up were recomputed independently from the raw tables, without the analysis scripts; all matched up to rounding, and the tuning rule recomputed from scratch chose the same settings.
  • One correction: the audit showed that most “collapses” disappeared after recomputing BatchNorm statistics (section 5), so the wording of the write-up was corrected.
  • Not checkable: the raw Tiny ImageNet tables are not in the repository, and the original study has no recalibrated evaluation.

Lessons and limits

If you deploy unlearning

  • Look at the class composition of deletion requests.
  • Test the method on concentrated requests: spread ones never showed the failure.
  • Report divergence, collapse, accuracy loss and forgetting strength separately.
  • Do not assume that tuning will save you.

What this does not show

  • No study measured true erasure or privacy.
  • BatchNorm is a suspect, not a proven mechanism.
  • Mostly one small CNN on Fashion-MNIST, with smaller checks on CIFAR-10 and Tiny ImageNet.
